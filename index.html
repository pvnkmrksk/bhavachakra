ಹೇಗೆ ಮಾಡಿದ್ದು how it was made

The lookup

Every English word on the wheel was sent to the rala search worker, one at a time, with a third of a second between calls and no X-Rala-Intent header, so none of it landed in the site's own search analytics.

GET https://rala-search.rala-search.workers.dev/?q=munisu
→ { query, count, results:[ {kannada, definition, type, source} ] }

Where it fell down

rala is built over Padakanaja, which is largely administrative, legal, scientific and agricultural. So stressed returned shear stress and tensile stress; confused returned a flour beetle; let down returned the dairy term for milk ejection; depressed returned "depressed classes". Words that failed as adjectives were retried as verb stems — annoyed → annoy — which recovered most of them.

Where it was right

On fear, anger and grief it is excellent, and better than a bilingual speaker's first instinct: seven graded fear words, four for doubt, and ತೇಜೋವಧೆ — "the murder of someone's lustre" — for humiliation. 73 of 130 entries here are rala's own answer, unaltered.

The reinterpretation

A translated wheel would say ಶಾಂತಿ for peaceful and ಅಸೂಯೆ for jealous. Both are dictionary-correct and neither is what gets said. Where the register was wrong, the spoken word won: ನೆಮ್ಮದಿ, ಹೊಟ್ಟೆಕಿಚ್ಚು, ಕೆಂಡಾಮಂಡಲ, ಬೇಜಾರು.

Word data from rala, a reversal of Alar by V. Krishna (ODC-ODbL) combined with Padakanaja, Government of Karnataka. Alar's own site was not queried. Wheel structure after Gloria Willcox's Feeling Wheel and its widely circulated three-ring descendant; the Kannada is a reinterpretation, not a translation of it.
